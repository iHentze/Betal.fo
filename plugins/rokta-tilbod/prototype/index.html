Ætlanin

Røkta tilboð

Betal turns a product on sale into a finished post for Facebook and Instagram. The shop owner never opens a blank design tool. Canva is not the product. It can be an "open to tweak" later, for someone who wants to move a logo.

What the owner sees

1. Tilboð
A list of what is on sale. Each row is the photo, the name, and both prices. The first version has no shop connection. "Nýtt tilboð" asks for a name, two prices, an end date, and a photo they already have.

2. Plakat
Three layouts of the same offer, in the shop's logo and colors. They choose a layout, not a new invention. Sizes:
- Instagram feed, 4:5, 1080×1350
- Facebook and Instagram square, 1:1, 1080×1080
- Story, 9:16, 1080×1920

The photo is theirs. The word TILBOÐ, the name, the old price, the new price, and how long the offer lasts are real text in the template. An image model must not paint the letters or the numbers.

3. Opslag
The poster sits in a phone frame, once as Instagram and once as Facebook. The caption is already written in Faroese and includes a Betal payment link.

Example:
Tilboð til fríggjadag. Lambalær, 179 kr í staðin fyri 249 kr. Keyp her: betal.fo/p/lambalaer

The actions are "Kopiera tekst" and "Tak niður myndir". Posting straight into Meta waits.

Why this is Betal
Canva can make a picture. It does not know the offer, it does not write Faroese, and it does not attach a payment link. The post should sell through the link.

Later
Shopify can feed the list of offers. An AI background is only for when they have no photo, and the price still stays in the template.

Not in the first version
Direct publishing, scheduling, and a shop connection.
